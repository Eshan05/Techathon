There is no single "total number" of farming laws in [Maharashtra](https://www.google.com/search?kgmid=/m/055vr&q=total+laws+related+to+farming+in+maharashtra+give+total+number+of+laws), as agriculture is governed by a combination of state-specific acts, central laws, and various regional regulations. However, there are approximately 10 to 15 major primary acts that form the core of the state's agricultural legal framework. [1, 2, 3, 4] 
## Core Agricultural Acts in Maharashtra
These primary laws regulate land ownership, marketing, and credit: [5] 

* Maharashtra Land Revenue Code, 1966: The foundational law governing land titles, classifications, and the assessment of land revenue.
* The Maharashtra Tenancy and Agricultural Lands Act, 1948 (Kul Kayda): Regulates the relationship between landlords and tenants and restricts land sales to "certified agriculturists" only.
* Maharashtra Agricultural Lands (Ceiling on Holdings) Act, 1961: Sets limits on the amount of land an individual or family can own to ensure equitable distribution.
* Maharashtra Agricultural Produce Marketing (Development and Regulation) Act, 1963: Governs the operation of [Agricultural Produce Market Committees (APMCs)](https://mahapanan.maharashtra.gov.in/SITE/Information/APMC.aspx) and the trading of farm produce.
* Maharashtra Prevention of Fragmentation and Consolidation of Holdings Act, 1947: Aims to prevent the excessive breakup of agricultural land into small, unviable fragments.
* The Maharashtra Agricultural Land Leasing Act, 2017 (Act No. 28 of 2023): A newer law enacted to facilitate formal land leasing while protecting the rights of both owners and cultivators. [2, 3, 5, 6, 7, 8, 9, 10] 

## Special and Support Laws

* Maharashtra Provision of Facilities for Agricultural Credit by Banks Act, 1974: Facilitates easier access to bank credit for farmers by removing certain restrictions on land alienation.
* Maharashtra Agricultural Pests and Diseases Act, 1947: Deals with the prevention and spread of agricultural pests and diseases.
* Maharashtra Agricultural Universities (Krishi Vidyapeeths) Act, 1983: Governs the establishment and functioning of agricultural research institutions. [1, 5, 11, 12] 

## Regional Variations
Due to the merger of different regions (Bombay, Vidarbha, and Hyderabad) into the modern state, some older regional acts still apply in specific areas: [5] 

* Maharashtra Tenancy and Agricultural Lands (Vidarbha Region) Act, 1958.
* Hyderabad Tenancy and Agricultural Lands Act, 1950 (applies to the Marathwada region). [13, 14] 

Note on Recent Changes: In 2021, the Maharashtra government introduced three bills—such as the Farmers Produce Trade and Commerce (Maharashtra Amendment) Bill—to modify the applicability of central farm laws within the state. [15, 16, 17]

While there is no official "fixed" count because laws are frequently amended or added, the legal framework for land in Maharashtra consists of approximately 10 to 12 major Acts supported by more than 40 sets of detailed Rules (sub-laws). [1] 
## Estimated Total of Primary Land Laws
There are roughly 12 primary state-level Acts that form the backbone of land governance. These are often categorized into revenue, tenancy, and land reform laws: [1] 

* The Maharashtra Land Revenue Code (MLRC), 1966: The "master law" that unified over [300 sections](https://lawcrust.in/maharashtra-land-revenue-code-1966/) of regional codes into one.
* Tenancy Laws: There are 3 regional variations (Bombay, Vidarbha, and Hyderabad Tenancy Acts) still in effect to address historical regional differences.
* Land Reform & Ceiling Laws: Key acts like the [Maharashtra Agricultural Lands (Ceiling on Holdings) Act, 1961](https://cdnbbsr.s3waas.gov.in/s36a4cbdaedcbda0fa8ddc7ea32073c475/uploads/2025/02/20250210859502930.pdf).
* Newer Acts: Includes the [Maharashtra Agricultural Land Leasing Act, 2017](https://prsindia.org/files/bills_acts/acts_states/maharashtra/2023/Act28of2023MH.pdf) (brought into force recently in 2023). [1, 2, 3, 4, 5] 

## Estimated Total of Sub-Laws (Rules)
The "sub-laws" or Rules are where the practical details (like how to fill out a 7/12 extract or how to apply for land conversion) are found. [1, 6] 

* MLRC Related Rules: There are at least 35 specific sets of rules derived directly from the MLRC alone.
* Examples of Critical Sub-Laws:
* Record of Rights Rules (1971): Governs the maintenance of 7/12 and 8A extracts.
   * Assessment & Settlement Rules (1970): Deals with how agricultural tax is calculated.
   * Boundary & Boundary Marks Rules (1969): Rules for physical land surveys.
   * Disposal of Government Lands Rules (1971): Rules for when the state leases or sells its own land. [1, 7, 8, 9] 

## Total Legislative Weight

* Primary State Acts: ~12
* Major Rules/Sub-laws: ~40+
* Sections of Law: The MLRC alone has over 300 sections, and the Tenancy Act has roughly 90 sections, meaning a farmer or landowner is governed by 400+ specific legal clauses. [1, 3]
